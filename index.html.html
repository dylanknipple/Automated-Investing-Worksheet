Understanding Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA) through S&P 500 Market Data

The Strategy: Automated Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA)

Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA) means setting up an automatic recurring payment to invest a fixed dollar amount (e.g., $200 on the 1st of every month) into the stock market—regardless of whether the market is going up or down.

I

Part I: Look at the Data

Review the 4-month automated investment history below, where $200 was invested on the 1st of each month into an S&P 500 index fund.

Month Share Price Monthly Contribution Shares Bought Total Shares Owned Total Money Invested Total Portfolio Value
Jan $50 $200 4.00 shares 4.00 shares $200 $200.00
Feb $41 $200 4.88 shares 8.88 shares $400 $364.08
Mar $37 $200 5.41 shares 14.29 shares $600 $528.73
Apr $65 $200 3.08 shares 17.37 shares $800 $1,129.05
1. Average Stock Market Price
$48.25

This is the simple average price of the stock over the 4 months ($50 + $41 + $37 + $65 ÷ 4). It shows what the stock cost on average if you just looked at the price tag each month.

2. Your DCA Average Cost Paid
$46.06

This is the actual price you paid per share using automated investing ($800 total spent ÷ 17.37 total shares owned).

Notice the difference! Your DCA average cost ($46.06) is lower than the market's average price ($48.25). Why? Because your $200 automatically bought more shares when the stock was cheap (in Feb and Mar) and fewer shares when it was expensive (in Apr).

Part III: Automated DCA vs. Other Strategies

Scenario A: Automated DCA vs. Single Lump Sum

Imagine you had $600 total in cash in January.

Strategy 1 (Lump Sum):

Spent all $600 at once in Jan at $50/share → Bought 12.00 shares.

Portfolio value in March ($37 price): $444.00 (Loss of $156.00)

Strategy 2 (Automated DCA):

Invested $200/mo in Jan, Feb, Mar → Accumulated 14.29 shares.

Portfolio value in March ($37 price): $528.73 (Loss of $71.27)

Scenario B: Automated DCA vs. "Timing the Market"

Imagine you kept $600 in cash in January out of fear, waiting for the "perfect moment". By April, the market spiked to $65/share. Panicking, you invested all $600 in April, getting only 9.23 shares. If you had used Automated DCA, you would already own 17.37 shares worth $1,129.05.
